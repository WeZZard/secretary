# Bridge on X

Use the `/communicate` skill. The goal is to carry one subject across a community boundary: a reader outside the subject's circle reads to the end and feels why it matters to them. This is not publicity, so do not announce, summarize the project's pitch or repeat its slogans.

**Before writing:** use the `/get-audience-on-x` skill and take its audience brief. The audience and the author's identity are fixed by that brief, not chosen by you. If its files are missing or stale, refresh them first. Write for that audience, as that author, in the author's own voice: read the author's recent original posts and match their wording, rhythm and length.

**Cover, in this order:**

1. **Hook.** Open with one sentence the audience already feels or half-believes, in their words rather than the source circle's jargon: a judgment, a tension or a surprise. It must make the reader want the second sentence.
2. **How it works.** Say what happens in everyday verbs, in the order it happens. Use only industry-standard nouns, never the project's own vocabulary. Anchor it to something the audience already knows by contrast: what it replaces, and what is different.
3. **Emotion.** Drive the whole post by feeling, not by facts: the relief, envy or stakes the reader would have if this were theirs. Push it once more at the end with the author's own stake and opinion, with at most one short hedge, then the link.

**MUST:**

1. You **MUST** keep it short: short paragraphs, one idea each.
2. You **MUST** label anything you have not verified, such as vendor claims, as the vendor's.
3. You **MUST** keep the feeling consistent with who the author is and speaks for, as the brief describes.

**MUST NOT:**

1. You **MUST NOT** use emoji, lists, bold, hashtags or signposts such as "here's how it works".
2. You **MUST NOT** put the author's private motives, backstory or grudges into the post.
3. You **MUST NOT** exaggerate: the emotion must come from a real stake, not from superlatives.
4. You **MUST NOT** choose or invent an audience, or spread anxiety or fear to move people.
